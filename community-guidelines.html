Community guidelines

Last updated: September 22, 2026

LOGR is a training log that other people can see. These are the rules that keep it worth opening. They sit alongside the Terms of Service, which is the binding version.

The short version

Post your own training. Be someone your teammates would want in the group chat. Nobody should have to think about whether opening LOGR is going to ruin their day.

What you might see here

Everything on LOGR was posted by another person. Captions, comments, workout notes, bios, usernames, messages, and photos are written and uploaded by users, and nobody reviews them before they appear. So you may run into text, photos, or any video and other media we add later that is explicit, offensive, or just wrong, even though none of it is allowed.

That is not us shrugging. It is why the tools below exist, why everything written here runs through our filters, and why a report gets looked at within a day. If you see something that breaks these rules, report it. We would much rather read one report too many than miss the one that mattered.

Do

  • Log honestly. A personal record only means something if the number behind it is real. Nobody is checking, which is exactly why it matters.
  • Give people credit. If you saved a routine from someone's post, it stays attributed to them. Leave it that way.
  • Encourage more than you critique. Unsolicited form advice on someone's first week back reads differently than you think it does.
  • Use the visibility settings. If a session is not for everyone, post it to followers or keep it private. That is what they are for.

Do not

  • Harass, threaten, or bully anyone, in posts, comments, or messages. This includes piling on, and it includes doing it as a joke.
  • Post hate speech or slurs, or attack people over race, ethnicity, religion, disability, sex, gender identity, or sexual orientation.
  • Impersonate someone else, or pretend to represent a team, school, or brand you have nothing to do with.
  • Post sexual content, graphic violence, or anything sexualising a minor. The last of these is reported to the authorities, not just removed.
  • Promote self-harm, disordered eating, or extreme cutting practices. Weight and body talk gets read by people in a worse place than you are. Log your numbers, do not moralise about anyone else's.
  • Sell things, spam, or run scams. No supplement affiliate links, no coaching funnels, no engagement farming.
  • Post training advice as medical advice. Share what you did, not what someone with an injury should do about it.
  • Share other people's photos or information without their say-so. A gym is full of people who did not agree to be in your post.

Tools you already have

  • Block. From someone's profile. It hides your content from them and theirs from you across posts, comments, profiles, and messages, and it removes any follow in both directions. You can undo it in Settings, under Blocked Accounts.
  • Report. Any post, comment, message, or account can be reported, with a reason. To report a message, press and hold it and choose Report; we keep a snapshot of the surrounding conversation, so the evidence survives even if the message is deleted afterwards.
  • Post visibility. Every post carries its own audience, and you can change it after posting.

Two filters also run on everything written here, on our servers rather than only in the app. Slurs, harassment, and threats are censored before the text is stored at all, on posts, captions, comments, messages, profile text, and community names alike. Nothing turns that off, and it cannot be undone later. Ordinary profanity is handled the other way round: it is stored as written and masked when it is shown to you, by a Family-Friendly Language filter that is on by default and that you can switch off for yourself in Settings.

What happens when someone breaks these

Depending on what happened and whether it is a pattern, we may remove the content, mute the account in a conversation, remove it from a team or community, or suspend or terminate it. Serious cases skip the earlier steps. Where it is practical and appropriate, we will tell the person why.

If you think we got it wrong, reply to us at support@getlogr.com and a person will look at it again.

Reporting something urgent

If you believe someone is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services first. We cannot respond at the speed an emergency needs. For anything involving a minor, or a credible threat, email support@getlogr.com with the usernames involved and we will prioritise it.
