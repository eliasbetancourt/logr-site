When Strava is enough

If your friends already run and ride on Strava and you lift a few times a week, its workout log keeps it all in one feed, and Strava says more than 200 million people use it. Its strength log is new, though, and sits inside an app built around runs and rides.

When a lifting app fits better

A lifting app is built around the gym: routines you repeat, PRs, and friends who lift. Hevy has the most lifters and the most devices, and can send each workout on to Strava, so your Strava friends still see it. LOGR is free with no subscription and built for lifting with friends and teams on iPhone, but it does not post to Strava. For the two of them in detail, see LOGR vs Hevy.

FAQ

Does Strava track weightlifting?

Yes. Since May 2026, Strava's workout log records sets, reps and weight, and each workout draws a map of the muscles it trained. Its coach-designed strength workouts, added in October 2026, are for subscribers, with one sample workout for free users.

Can a lifting app send my workouts to Strava?

Some can. Strava named 14 strength partners in May 2026, Hevy among them, and Hevy sends each finished workout to Strava. LOGR does not post to Strava.

Is LOGR free?

Yes. LOGR is free, with no subscription, no ads, and none of your history behind a paywall.

Is LOGR on Android?

No. LOGR is an iPhone app, for iOS 15.1 or later.
